About this prototype

What this is

A click-through of Intent AI inside today’s Intent One shell (menu rail, chat column, canvas). v1.0 reworks the 8 September version after the behavioural token session with Sangram. The order still matches the build order: predicted behaviours first, then data story enrichment, then per-person split assignment, then simulation.

The Intent AI tag marks where the model is doing the work.

What changed from v0.9

  • The space is predicted behaviour, not audiences. People are placed by the tokens the model predicts for them next, so a cluster is “what this group is about to do”. Inside a cluster, people split by predicted next event.
  • The behavioural token is on screen. Every event in a person’s sequence is shown as four slots plus time.
  • Simulate is a fourth scene. Predicted behaviour with and without the campaign, before activating.
  • Accuracy over the last 7 days is shown wherever a prediction is shown.
  • Synthetic telco (weblog + Edge SDK + CRM profile) replaces synthetic retail.

The behavioural token

Any behaviour from any source is written into four slots plus time (when it started, how long it lasted). Each slot value comes from one shared vocabulary with fixed IDs, and each value is only allowed in its own slot, so the model knows whether it is predicting a brand or a category. Because every source lands in the same shape, one encoder covers all of them.

Data status

All numbers are synthetic and illustrative. The operator, the clusters, the tokens, the shares, the accuracy figures and the simulation are placeholders shaped like what the model will output. Nothing here is a live model read.

As described in the session: the vocabulary (with similarity between terms taken from an existing text encoder), the tokenizer and the CRM profile encoder exist. The behaviour model is not trained yet. It will be trained first on synthetic profiles with agent-generated day-by-day behaviour, with encoder and sequence prediction optimised together. Simulation comes after.

Decisions this version forces

  • Horizon. This prototype shows behaviour over the next 30 days. The model as described predicts the next tokens, which is hours. Getting from hours to weeks means rolling predictions forward or predicting a summary, and accuracy will fall with distance. The horizon the product promises should follow what the model can hold, which is not known yet.
  • Slot D. Where, device and price bucket are competing for the same slot across sources. One namespace has to be chosen and kept.
  • Weblog-only clients. Without the SDK, two of four slots are unknown. An LLM could fill them, but that is synthetic and adds little. Tests whether weblog alone is enough to sell this.
  • Showing tokens to clients. Sangram’s view: the four-slot structure is not IP and works as a sales explanation; how a token becomes an embedding is IP. This prototype follows that.
  • Training on client weblogs in place. Proposal is to train inside the client environment (MTN) and not take the model weights out. Whether the contract allows even that needs checking before it is promised.
  • Predicted behaviours vs Moments. A goal-agnostic predicted behaviour is close to a Moment in Goals → Packs → Moments → Campaigns. New object, or the Moments layer?

Carried over from v0.9

Prediction is per person; activation is per group. A person is a hash ID with a token sequence and a predicted next token. No individual is addressed individually and no per-user content is generated. Accepted for the prototype, not resolved as a product principle.

Discovery is decoupled from goals; activation is not. Intent AI suggests a goal from the predicted next event; it is confirmed when the opportunity is created, and relevance and value are scored at that point.

A free-text ask is not a cluster. The description is embedded and the nearest region of the space is lit. It can cut across clusters; the agent says which and how many people sit outside any.

What each scene needs from the model track

  • 1 Predicted behaviours: tokenizer, encoder and next-token prediction, plus clustering of the predicted tokens across the base.
  • 2 Data story: the same predictions and brand affinity per hash ID, aggregated and written into the data story prompt.
  • 3 Opportunity splits: policy layer scoring each person under each split.
  • 4 Simulate: campaign exposure captured as an intervention token, and enough past campaigns to learn from. Last to be built.

Not in this prototype

  • Scenes 2 to 4 are mocked for one cluster only (comparing networks).
  • Creatives, activation settings and performance tabs are unchanged and not mocked.
  • Privacy controls (opt-out, retention, audit) beyond the stated boundary.
  • How Sam’s team chooses between the top-down and bottom-up path.
